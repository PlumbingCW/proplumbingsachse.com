Pipe Lining Sewer Repair: A Sachse, TX Homeowner Guide

Pipe lining repairs a damaged sewer line from the inside. It can save a yard and a driveway, but it only works on the right kind of damage.

By the Sachse Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 2026
Talk to a plumber
(972) 903-8224
Call Now

When a sewer line cracks, roots get in, or a joint separates, the traditional fix was to dig a trench along the pipe and replace it. Pipe lining is an alternative. A flexible liner soaked in resin is pulled or inflated inside the old pipe, then cured so it hardens into a new pipe within the old one.

This guide explains how lining works, what it can and cannot fix and what to ask for before you agree to it.

How pipe lining works

1

Camera inspection

A camera records the pipe so the plumber can see the damage and measure the run.

2

Cleaning

The pipe is cleaned with a cable or by hydro jetting so the liner bonds to a clean wall.

3

Install the liner

A resin-coated liner is pulled into place and inflated against the old pipe.

4

Cure and cut open

The resin hardens, and the plumber reopens the branch lines and checks the result with the camera.

Because the work happens through existing cleanouts or small access holes, the yard, landscaping and driveway stay mostly intact.

What lining can fix

  • Cracks and small fractures along the length of the pipe
  • Joints where roots enter
  • Pipe that has worn thin or corroded on the inside
  • Offset joints that are mild

What lining cannot fix

  • A pipe that has collapsed or is badly crushed
  • A severe belly where the pipe sags and holds water
  • A pipe with a large missing section
  • A line the liner cannot pass because of a tight bend

In those cases, excavation or a pipe bursting method is needed. A plumber who recommends lining for every damaged line is not reading the camera.

Lining, spot repair or replacement

Condition on cameraUsual answer
Roots at one jointCut roots, then spot repair or a short liner
Cracks along one runLiner
Collapsed sectionDig and replace that section
Belly holding waterDig and regrade the section
Whole line failingFull replacement or a long liner

Questions to ask your plumber

  • What did the camera show, and can I see the video?
  • Is lining suited to this pipe, or do you recommend digging?
  • What material is the liner, and what is the warranty?
  • Will the branch lines be reopened and checked?
  • Does the price include a final camera inspection?

We do not state the age or material of Sachse sewer lines, because that varies by street and by when the house was built. The camera shows what you have.

Signs your sewer line needs attention

Slow drains in more than one fixture, gurgling sounds, sewage smell in the yard and soggy patches over the line are the usual signs. If you have these, sewer line cleaning may clear a blockage and the camera will show if damage is behind it. Homes in Wylie and Murphy get the same inspection process.

How lining compares with digging

Digging gives the plumber full access and a brand new pipe, and it is the right answer when the old pipe has collapsed or sags badly. The cost is a trench through your yard, possibly through a driveway or a tree root zone, and the restoration that follows. Lining leaves most of that alone, but it works only inside a pipe that still has its shape.

The liner also reduces the inside diameter of the pipe a little, since it adds a layer. In most residential lines that is not a problem, but a plumber should confirm that the line will still carry the flow from your house.

After the work is done

Ask for the video of the finished pipe and for the warranty in writing. Keep both with your house records. If you sell the house, a buyer or an inspector may ask for them, and a clean after-video is easy proof that the work was done properly.

If you plan to sell, lining can be a good selling point because the work is recorded on video and the warranty can be passed to the new owner when the terms allow it. Read the warranty to see whether it transfers.

Signs lining may suit your line

A line that clogs again and again at the same joint, a camera that shows fine cracks or roots with no collapse and a pipe that still holds its round shape are all good signs. A shorter run with one or two access points is easier to line than a long run with many bends.

Common questions

Does pipe lining need a trench?

Usually not. The liner goes in through an existing cleanout or a small access hole.

How long does a liner last?

Manufacturers state a long service life, and a plumber should give the warranty in writing.

Can a lined pipe still get roots?

The liner seals the joints roots use, so it reduces the chance.
