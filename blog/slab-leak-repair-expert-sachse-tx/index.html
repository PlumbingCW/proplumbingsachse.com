How to Choose a Slab Leak Repair Expert in Sachse, TX

A slab leak needs a plumber who finds the leak before cutting. This guide explains what a good process looks like and what to ask.

By the Sachse Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 2026
Talk to a plumber
(972) 903-8224
Call Now

Slab leak calls attract a wide range of businesses, and the quality varies. Some plumbers locate the leak with equipment and repair it at the exact spot. Others cut at a guess. The difference decides how much of your floor is opened and how much you pay.

This page is about choosing the plumber. For what a slab leak is and how it is repaired, see our slab leak repair page.

What an expert does before touching the floor

1

Confirms the leak

A meter test with every fixture off shows whether water is moving through the house line.

2

Isolates the line

The plumber closes valves in turn to narrow the leak to hot or cold and to one part of the house.

3

Locates the spot

Electronic listening and tracing tools pin the leak to a small area of the slab.

4

Offers choices

Spot repair, rerouting or repiping are compared with a written price.

A plumber who goes straight to cutting without locating is guessing, and a guess opens more of your floor than necessary.

Questions worth asking

  • How will you find the leak before cutting?
  • What are my options besides breaking the slab?
  • Is the price in writing, and what does it include?
  • Who repairs the flooring and the concrete?
  • Will the work be permitted and inspected?
  • Are you licensed, and can I check it?

Red flags

  • A price quoted over the phone without any testing
  • Pressure to decide on the spot
  • No plan for flooring or finish work
  • A recommendation to repipe the whole house after one test
  • No clear answer on licensing

A repipe can be the right answer. A repipe recommended before any testing is not an answer, it is a sales pitch.

Licensing and permits in Sachse

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners license lookup lets you check any plumber who gives you a quote. Ask for the license number in writing and look it up before work starts.

For permits and inspections on slab work in Sachse, ask the City of Sachse building department. The main line is 972.495.1212.

Insurance and documentation

Many homeowner policies cover water damage that follows a sudden leak, but not the cost of finding or fixing the pipe. A written report from the plumber that explains where the leak was and what caused it helps your claim. Ask for photographs and a copy of the invoice.

After the repair

Watch the meter with every fixture off for a few hours after the repair. It should stop. If it still moves, call the plumber back right away. A second leak is possible on pipe of the same age.

Garland homes built in the 1960s and 1970s see this pattern more often, as we explain on our Garland page. Murphy houses are newer, and the Murphy page explains what we see there.

What a clear estimate includes

A written estimate for a slab leak should say where the leak is, which option is proposed, what the price covers and what it does not. It should name the flooring and the concrete work, state who is responsible for them and describe what happens if the plumber finds a second leak during the repair.

Protecting your home during the work

  • Ask for floor protection in the path from the door to the work area
  • Move furniture and rugs out of the room before the crew arrives
  • Ask about dust control when concrete is cut
  • Confirm how the water will be shut off and for how long

A good crew explains the plan before it starts and cleans up when it is done. If a plumber is vague about either one, treat that as information.

Ask what the plumber will do if the repair uncovers a problem that was not visible on the first test. A clear answer, such as a stop and a call to you before extra work, protects both sides and avoids surprise charges.

How long the repair takes

Locating a slab leak is usually quick. The repair itself takes anywhere from a few hours for a simple spot fix to a few days for a reroute or a repipe, plus drying and finish time for floors. Ask for a schedule that names each stage, and ask what happens if the crew finds a second leak.

Safety steps while you wait

  • Close the main water valve if water is visible.
  • Stay off wet floors near outlets and switch off power to the area from the breaker.
  • Move belongings off the floor and photograph damage for your insurer.
  • Do not cut into the slab yourself.

Common questions

How is a slab leak found?

A meter test confirms the leak, then valves isolate it and electronic tools pin the spot before any cutting.

Is a slab leak an emergency?

It can be. Water under the house can damage flooring and the foundation, so call promptly and close the main if water is visible.

Does insurance pay for slab leaks?

Policies vary. Many pay for resulting water damage but not for the pipe repair. Check your policy.

How do I check a Texas plumber license?

Use the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners license lookup.

Do slab repairs need a permit in Sachse?

Ask the City of Sachse building department. Rules depend on the work.
