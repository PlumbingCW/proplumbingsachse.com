Toilet Tank Installation in Sachse, TX

Replacing a toilet tank is a small job on paper. The details are the tank bolts, the gasket and a matching bowl, and they decide whether it stays dry.

By the Sachse Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

Toilets come in two main shapes. A one-piece toilet has the tank and bowl molded together. A two-piece toilet has a separate tank that sits on the bowl with a gasket and two or three bolts. When people say they need a new tank, they mean a two-piece toilet whose tank is cracked, leaking or worn out inside.

This guide explains when new tank parts are enough, when a new tank is the answer and when replacing the whole toilet is the better choice for a Sachse home.

Parts that fail inside the tank

Flapper

A worn rubber flapper lets water seep into the bowl, so the toilet runs or refills on its own.

Fill valve

A fill valve that sticks or whistles keeps the tank from shutting off at the right level.

Flush handle and chain

A chain that is too short or too long keeps the flapper from seating.

Tank bolts and gasket

Rusted bolts and a hardened gasket let water drip from the tank base.

These parts cost little and are replaced in most visits. If the tank itself is sound, new parts are the answer.

When the tank itself is the problem

A hairline crack in the tank shows as a damp line, or a slow leak down the outside. Porcelain cannot be patched in a way that lasts. If the crack is above the waterline, a leak may be slow. If it is below, the tank empties and the toilet cannot flush. Replace the tank, or the whole toilet.

Can I buy just a tank?

Sometimes. The tank must match the bowl in make, model and the bolt pattern. Older toilets may have tanks that are no longer made. When a match cannot be found, replacing the toilet is cleaner than adapting parts, and the new unit often uses less water per flush.

How a tank is replaced

1

Shut the water and drain the tank

Close the valve behind the toilet, flush and sponge out the rest.

2

Disconnect the supply line

Unscrew the line from the fill valve, with a towel to catch drips.

3

Remove the old tank

Unscrew the tank bolts under the bowl rim and lift the tank straight up. Porcelain is heavy and breakable.

4

Set the new tank

Place the new gasket, seat the tank level on the bowl and tighten the bolts a little at a time, alternating sides.

5

Reconnect and test

Connect the supply line, open the valve, fill and flush while checking every joint for drips.

Over-tightening is the common mistake. It cracks the porcelain. The goal is snug and level.

Repair, new tank or new toilet

SituationBest answer
Running or weak flushNew flapper and fill valve
Drip at the boltsNew bolts and gasket

Toilets in Sachse homes

Sachse sits on expansive clay soils, according to the Dallas County Master Gardeners, and slabs can shift slightly with changes in moisture. A toilet that starts to rock a little may be showing small movement in the floor and not only a loose bolt.

If a new toilet will not sit level even after the closet bolts are tightened, ask a plumber to check the flange and the floor under it.

When to call a plumber

Call when the toilet leaks at the base, when water is coming through a ceiling below, when you cannot find a matching part or when the shutoff valve will not close. For stubborn clogs that repeat, see our drain cleaning page. Calls from Murphy and Garland come to the same 24/7 line.

Choosing a replacement tank or toilet

Check three things before you buy. The rough-in distance is the measure from the wall to the center of the floor flange, and 12 inches is the common size. The bowl shape is round or elongated, and it must match the seat you own or plan to buy. The flush system matters too, since a gravity flush is quiet and simple while a pressure-assisted unit is louder and stronger.

If you take the old tank or toilet to the store, the model number stamped inside the tank lid or under the tank helps staff find the right match. Photos of the bolt pattern help as well.

Common questions

Can I replace just the toilet tank?

Often yes, if the tank is for a two-piece toilet and a matching model is available. Otherwise replace the toilet.

Why is water dripping from the tank bolts?

The rubber washers or the gasket have worn. New bolts and a gasket usually fix it.

How do I stop a toilet that keeps running?

Check the flapper first, then the fill valve and the chain length.

Is my toilet leaking from the tank or the base?

Dry everything, put tissue around the joints and flush. The spot where it gets wet shows the source.

Does Sachse soil affect toilet installation?

Slab movement can affect how level a toilet sits. A plumber checks the flange and the floor if a toilet will not stay stable.
