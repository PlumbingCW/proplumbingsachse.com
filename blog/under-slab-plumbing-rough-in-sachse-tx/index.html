Under Slab Plumbing Rough In in Sachse, TX

Under slab rough in is the one plumbing stage you cannot easily change later. Once the concrete is poured, the drain locations are fixed.

By the Sachse Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 2026

Call Now

When a house or an addition is built on a concrete slab, the first plumbing is installed in the ground before the concrete goes down. This is called under slab rough in. It includes the drain, waste and vent lines that carry water out, and sometimes water supply lines or sleeves that will be used later.

Mistakes at this stage are expensive, because fixing them means cutting concrete. This guide explains what happens, what to check and what a Sachse homeowner or builder should ask.

What goes in before the pour

Drain lines

Pipes from each toilet, tub, shower, sink and floor drain run to the main sewer line with a steady downward slope.

Vent connections

Vent pipes stubbed up through the walls keep drains flowing and keep sewer gas out of the house.

Cleanouts

Access points that let a future plumber reach the line without digging.

Sleeves and stub-outs

Pipes or sleeves left in the slab for lines that will be run later.

Why location matters so much

Fixtures must land where the plans say they will. A toilet flange off by two inches changes how the bathroom works. A shower drain that is not centered in the shower pan means a custom fix. Measure from the framing plan, mark the drain points on the ground and confirm them before the pipes are glued in.

Slope, bedding and testing

Drain pipes need a consistent slope so waste flows and does not settle. The code sets a required slope, and the City of Sachse building department can tell you which code applies and what it requires for your project. Pipes are also supported and bedded so the weight of the slab does not push them out of line.

Before the concrete is poured, the system is tested. A water or air test fills the drain pipes and watches the level or pressure. If it drops, there is a leak. The inspector may need to see the test in place before the pour is allowed.

The sequence on a typical job

1

Plan and mark

Fixture locations are drawn from the plan and marked on the prepared ground.

2

Trench and lay

The plumber digs trenches and lays drain pipe with the right slope and support.

3

Test

The system is filled or pressurized to find leaks.

4

Inspect

The inspector reviews the work and the test before the pour.

5

Protect

Open pipe ends are capped and the stub-ups are protected during the pour.

Expansive soil and slabs

Slab plumbing in Sachse

The Dallas County Master Gardeners describe Sachse soils as expansive clay, which swells when wet and shrinks when dry. That movement is why slab foundations in this area are engineered and why plumbing in or under a slab is installed with care. Ask your builder or engineer how the slab is designed.

Permits and inspections for new plumbing are handled by the City of Sachse building department. Ask which inspections apply, and when.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Setting drain locations from memory and not from the plan
  • Pouring before the test and the inspection are complete
  • Leaving no cleanout near the main exit of the house
  • Skipping a sleeve for a future line such as a laundry sink or a wet bar
  • Letting pipe ends fill with soil or debris

After the slab is poured

Once the slab is poured, the finish plumbing comes in stages. First the supply lines and vents are completed in the walls, then fixtures are set. If you later need to move a fixture, it may mean cutting the slab, so choose locations carefully. Our plumbing installation page explains the stages above the slab, and leak detection explains what to do if a slab line ever fails.

Builders and homeowners in Murphy and Wylie face the same slab questions, because new construction there is active.

Questions for your builder or plumber

  • Where will the main sewer exit the house, and where is the cleanout?
  • Will the drain test be recorded or witnessed by the inspector?
  • Which fixtures are planned now, and which might be added later?
  • What pipe material is used for the drains and for any sleeves?

Write the answers into the plan or the contract. Memory is a poor record when a pour is scheduled for early the next morning.

Take dated photos of the open trenches before the pour. Once the concrete is down, those pictures are the only record of where the pipes run, and they help any future plumber who needs to reach them.

Add-ons to plan now

Think about a laundry sink, a wet bar, a floor drain in the garage and an extra half bath before the pour. A sleeve or a capped stub costs little now and saves a concrete cut later.

Common questions

What is under slab plumbing rough in?

It is the drain, waste and vent piping installed in the ground before a slab is poured.

Can I move a drain after the slab is poured?

Only by cutting the slab. That is why locations are confirmed before the pour.

Who inspects the work in Sachse?

The City of Sachse building department. Ask which inspections apply to your project.

How is the system tested?

It is filled with water or pressurized with air and watched for a drop.

Why does Sachse soil matter for slab plumbing?

Expansive clay moves with moisture, so slabs are engineered and pipes are supported and bedded with care.
