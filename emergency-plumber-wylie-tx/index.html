Wylie is a town of two ages

Wylie was incorporated in 1887 and was first called Nickelville, after its first store. A tornado hit downtown on May 9, 1993, and two fires in December 1998 damaged several businesses. Downtown was renovated afterward and many century-old buildings were kept. Wylie is also one of the fastest-growing cities in the area, rising from about 41,400 residents in 2010 to about 57,500 in 2020.

That mix matters for plumbing. Older downtown buildings have older drain and supply lines, and the newer neighborhoods have homes that are only now meeting their first water heater replacement. Our 24/7 emergency line serves both, and a licensed plumber is booked for the earliest slot available.

Drains and sewer lines lead the calls in Wylie

The Wylie requests that come in most often start with a drain. A sink that will not empty, a floor drain with standing water or a toilet that bubbles when the washing machine runs usually points to a clog in the branch line or the main. A cable machine clears most of them. If the problem comes back, a camera shows what is wrong, and hydro jetting cleans heavy grease and scale from the pipe wall.

For a backup that affects several fixtures at once, read our sewer line cleaning page. If you suspect a break or a belly in the pipe, sewer inspection explains how the camera works.

Water in Wylie

Lavon Dam and Lake Lavon lie about five miles north of town, and the North Texas Municipal Water District has its offices in Wylie. The district supplies treated water to many cities in the area, and the City of Murphy confirms that it buys from it.
