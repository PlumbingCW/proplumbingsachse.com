Hydro jetting is high-pressure water, delivered through a rotating nozzle, that scours the inside of a drain or sewer line. It is the job to ask about when a cable opens the line and the clog returns within weeks.

Some of those repeat clogs become urgent. A main line packed with grease and roots can back up into a tub or floor drain at night, and our 24/7 emergency line is open for those calls. Stop using water, keep valuables off the floor and tell the dispatcher what is backing up.

Hydro jetting in Sachse, TX starts with a diagnosis. A jetter cleans sound pipe well. In broken or offset pipe it can make a bad situation worse, so a camera look comes first.

When a main line backs up and you suspect grease or roots

Stop all water use

Washing machines and dishwashers push dozens of gallons into a line that is already full.

Locate the cleanout

The cleanout is the access point the plumber will use. Clear mulch or a planter off the cap if you can do it safely.

Keep children and pets out

Backed-up wastewater is a health hazard. Keep the area dry and closed off until the line is cleared and the floor is cleaned.

What hydro jetting actually does

A jetting hose with a forward and backward spraying nozzle is fed into the line. Residential units commonly run between roughly 3,000 and 4,000 PSI, and the nozzle pulls itself forward while it cuts grease and scale off the pipe wall.

The water carries the debris downstream to the sewer main. A cable punches a hole through a clog. Jetting restores the pipe to nearly full diameter, which is why a jetted line often stays clear for much longer.

When we recommend a camera first

Pressurized water finds weak spots. A cracked clay or cast iron line, a belly full of standing water or a section that has pulled apart can all be damaged further by a jetting run. A sewer camera inspection shows the pipe condition before any water goes in, and it often shows why the clogs keep coming back.

Grease, scale and roots in Sachse lines

Grease is the most common reason a house line needs jetting. It cools in the pipe and builds a layer that traps wipes and paper. Cast iron and older steel pipe can also form rough scale inside, and that scale catches everything.

Roots are the other regular cause. A mature tree near a front yard cleanout can send hair-thin roots into a joint. A cable cuts them back. Jetting clears the root hairs and the debris around them, but if the joint is open, the line may eventually need a sewer line repair to stop re-entry.

Safety steps around jetting equipment

  • Keep children and pets away from the hose and the cleanout.
  • Never touch the jet stream, which can cut skin.
  • Keep toilets and sinks unused while the line is being cleaned.
  • Stand clear of the cleanout when the line is first opened.
Ask your plumber for the camera video before and after jetting, and look up their license with the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.

Common questions

Do you handle hydro jetting emergencies at night or on weekends in Sachse?

Yes. The 24/7 line answers on weekends and after hours. If a main line has backed up into a tub or floor drain, call right away and say that the water is rising, because the plumber may open the line with a cable first and schedule jetting after a camera check.

Will hydro jetting damage my pipes?

On sound pipe it will not. On cracked, offset or collapsed pipe, it can. That is why a camera inspection comes first when there is any doubt.

How long does a jetting visit take?

It depends on pipe length and how much buildup is there. Many jobs fit in a few hours, and a heavily coated line takes longer. Ask the dispatcher for a realistic window.

How often should a line be jetted?

Only when it needs it. A home with a one-time clog may never need jetting. A line with a grease or root pattern may be put on a yearly check.

Is jetting safe for old cast iron?

Often yes, with a camera check first. Scale inside cast iron comes off under jetting, but a thin or cracked wall can leak once it is cleaned, so the plumber should tell you what they see.
