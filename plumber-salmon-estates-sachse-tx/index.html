A short history of the town around it

Sachse began in 1886 when William Sachse donated land for a Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe railroad line, and the railroad named the stop for him. Residents voted to incorporate on April 14, 1956. Incorporation made the town eligible for water from the North Texas Municipal Water District.

That timeline helps explain why Sachse has housing from several eras. We do not have a single construction date for the whole of Salmon Estates, and the sections were platted at different times.

Older does not tell you what is behind the wall

One listing in Salmon Estates shows a house built in 1986 on a lot of about one acre. We cannot say that applies to every home here, and a house's age does not tell you what pipe is inside it. Owners, builders and past repairs all change the answer.

So the honest approach is to look. A plumber can check visible supply lines and shutoff valves, run a camera through the sewer line and test for leaks at the meter. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what that shows.

  1. Look at the water meter with every fixture off. A moving dial points to a leak.
  2. Check under sinks and around the water heater for drips, stains or corrosion.
  3. Note slow drains, gurgling or odors and which fixtures are involved.
  4. Locate the main shutoff valve and the sewer cleanout.
  5. Call with what you found so the plumber knows where to start.

Questions that fit older homes

If you see discolored water, low pressure that worsens over time or repeated leaks in different spots, ask about repiping before you pay for several separate repairs. Repiping is not a default answer. It is one option to compare with targeted repair once someone has looked at the system.

For stubborn slow drains, drain cleaning comes first, followed by a camera if the problem keeps returning. A leak you cannot find is a job for leak detection.

Larger lots and long lines

Some Salmon Estates lots are far larger than a typical subdivision lot. A longer run from the house to the street means more pipe that you cannot see. If you ever have a wet spot in the yard that does not dry, call before it grows.

Common questions

The city subdivision map lists Salmon Estates in numbered sections in the 75048 area, with homes on streets such as Sachse Road and Salmon Street. We do not have an exact boundary, so call with your street.

How old are homes in Salmon Estates?

Does an older house need repiping?

Not automatically. Age alone does not decide it. A plumber has to look at the pipe, the leak history and the water pressure before suggesting it.

Can a camera look at my sewer line?

Yes. A camera inspection shows the condition of the private line from the cleanout toward the street and records what it finds.
