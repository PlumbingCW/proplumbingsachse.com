Plumbing installation is planned work, which is why it works best when it is scheduled early. A bathroom remodel, a kitchen update, a laundry relocation or a new addition all depend on supply lines, drains and vents that fit the plan and pass inspection.

Plumbing installation in Sachse, TX covers new fixtures, supply and drain piping, rough-in for additions and new construction, PEX supply systems, sink drains and toilet installation, and the shutoff valves that go with them.

What installation covers

Fixtures

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers and disposals are set, connected and tested.

Supply lines

New water lines run from the main or a branch to each fixture, usually in PEX or copper.

Drain and vent lines

Waste lines slope to the sewer, and vents let the system breathe.

Appliance connections

Dishwashers, ice makers, washing machines and water heaters need shutoffs and drains.

Rough-in and finish

Rough-in is the first stage, when pipes are installed inside open walls and under floors, before drywall. Finish is the second stage, when fixtures are set and connected. The two stages are separated by inspections and by the other trades. Under slab rough-in, in new construction, sets the drain locations before concrete is poured and cannot be changed afterward, so measurements must be right.

Common questions

Do you do new construction plumbing?

We handle plumbing installation for additions and remodels, and rough-in for small projects. Ask on the call about the size and scope of yours.

Do I need a permit?

Often, but the rules vary. Ask the City of Sachse building department, and the plumber will say what is usual.

Can you install fixtures I bought?

Yes, in most cases. Bring the model number so the plumber can check the fit.

How far ahead should I schedule?

As early as you can. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, but planned work goes more smoothly with notice.

Do you move existing plumbing?

Yes. Moving a sink or a toilet depends on the drain slope and the vent, and the plumber checks those first.
