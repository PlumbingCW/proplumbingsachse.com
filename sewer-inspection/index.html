A sewer inspection is a video look inside the line that runs from your house to the sewer connection. A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable goes in through a cleanout, and the plumber watches the pipe wall on a monitor as it moves.

Sewer inspection in Sachse, TX is most useful at three moments: before you buy a home, after a clog that has come back more than once, and before any repair that might involve digging. In each case the camera replaces a guess with something you can see.

What a sewer camera can find

Cracks and broken sections

The camera shows the shape of the pipe wall and where it has split or shifted.

Root entry

Fine roots at a joint look like white hair. A heavy mass looks like a plug.

Belly sections

A sag that holds water shows as a pool in the pipe that does not drain.

Offset joints and scale

Two pipe sections that no longer line up, or rough buildup on iron pipe, are easy to spot.

How the inspection works

1

Find access

The plumber opens the outdoor cleanout or pulls a toilet when no cleanout exists.

2

3

Locate problems

A transmitter in the camera head lets the plumber trace and mark the spot above ground.

4

Review the video

You see the same footage, and we explain what it means for repair or maintenance.

Buying a home in Sachse: why the line matters

A home inspector looks at what is above ground. The sewer line is underground, so a clog-free test flush says very little about a cracked pipe. A camera inspection before closing can reveal root damage or a bellied line, and it gives you information to use when you negotiate the repair or the price.

Repeat clogs and the camera

If a drain cleaning visit clears the line but the problem returns, the camera tells you why. A line that is only coated with grease may be a good fit for hydro jetting. A line with a crack or an offset needs a repair, and jetting could make it worse.

Sachse neighborhoods and what the camera sees

A camera run in Woodbridge, Heritage Park, Sachse Farms, Jackson Meadows or anywhere else in Sachse covers the private line from your cleanout toward the street connection. The camera cannot see into the city main, so a problem that appears to start under the street should be reported to the City of Sachse.

Common questions

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most inspections take about an hour on a clear line. A line that needs cleaning first or a long run takes longer.

Do I need to be home?

Someone should be present at the start to point out the cleanout and at the end to review the video.

Can the camera see into the city main?

It sees the private line from your cleanout toward the street. A problem under the street is the city's, and the City of Sachse is the right contact for it.

Will I get a copy of the video?

Ask when you book. A copy of the footage is useful for a repair estimate or a home purchase.

Can you inspect a line with no cleanout?

Often yes. The plumber can pull a toilet and put the camera in there, and may suggest installing a cleanout while they are at it.
