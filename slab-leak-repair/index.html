A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or through the concrete foundation. It is out of sight, so people first notice the water bill, a warm patch on the floor or a faint sound of running water at night. When the leak is active and water is surfacing, it is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it.

Slab leak repair in Sachse, TX starts by confirming the leak and its location, then offering repair options that fit your house. Until the plumber arrives, shut off the main water valve to stop the loss.

Slab leak emergency: what to do first

Shut off the main valve

This stops the water feeding the leak. Test a faucet to confirm the flow has stopped.

Turn off the water heater

If the leak is on the hot line, shut off power or gas to the heater so it does not run dry.

Move belongings

Lift rugs and boxes off wet floors, and keep furniture off damp carpet.

Look for new cracks

Photograph any cracks, wet flooring or warm spots so the plumber can compare them.

Signs of a slab leak

A warm spot on the floor

A leaking hot water line heats the concrete above it.

Running water sound with fixtures off

A hiss or trickle in a quiet house suggests water moving under pressure.

A sudden high bill or a spinning meter

The meter moves when every fixture is off.

Wet carpet, damp baseboards or a musty smell

Water that reaches the surface wets finishes and invites mold.

Why slab leaks happen in the Dallas area

The soil under much of the Dallas area is expansive clay, the kind the Dallas County Master Gardeners describe as swelling and shrinking with moisture. As the soil moves, a slab can shift a little, and the pipes inside or below it rub, bend and wear.

Water chemistry, pipe age and rubbing against gravel or concrete add to it. A leak makes the problem worse, because extra water under a house moves the soil even more.

How a slab leak is confirmed

The plumber closes the fixtures and checks the meter, isolates sections of the supply line with pressure tests and listens with acoustic equipment. A thermal imaging camera can show a hot water leak as a warm area on the floor. The goal is to find the spot within a small area before any concrete is cut. Living with a slab leak while you decide

Many homeowners shut off the main valve at night and run the water by day until a repair is scheduled. That reduces loss and limits damage while you choose between options. Do not ignore a slab leak. The water can soften the soil under the foundation and the bill keeps rising.

Common questions

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Sachse?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour. Shut off the main water valve first, then call and describe what you see, and the plumber is booked for the earliest available slot.

How do I know it is a slab leak and not a pipe leak elsewhere?

A warm floor spot, a running sound with fixtures off and a meter that moves are strong clues. The plumber confirms with a pressure test and acoustic tools.

Does a slab leak mean my foundation is damaged?

Not by itself. A long-running leak can soften the soil and contribute to movement, which is why prompt repair matters. A structural engineer can judge the foundation.

Will you have to break my floor?

Only if a spot repair is the chosen path. Rerouting through the attic avoids the slab, and the plumber explains the trade-offs before work starts.

Can a slab leak fix itself?

No. Leaks grow. Delaying repair increases the water loss and the damage.
