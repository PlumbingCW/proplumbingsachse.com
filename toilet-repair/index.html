If water is rising, turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the refill, and lift the tank lid to close the flapper by hand.

Overflowing toilet: what to do first

Close the shutoff valve

The valve sits on the wall or floor behind the toilet. Turning it clockwise stops water from entering the tank.

Lift the flapper

Reaching into the tank and pushing the flapper shut stops water entering the bowl.

Protect the floor

Towels and a bucket slow the spread. Do not flush again.

Check other fixtures

If a tub or sink is also backing up, the clog is in a shared line, not just the toilet.

Common toilet problems

Clogged toilet

A plunger or a closet auger clears most toilet clogs. Repeat clogs suggest a deeper blockage or a vent problem.

Running toilet

A worn flapper, a bad fill valve or a chain that is too tight wastes water and raises the bill.

Leak at the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts let water seep onto the floor.

Rocking or loose toilet

A cracked flange or an uneven floor lets the bowl shift and breaks the seal.

Flanges and wax rings

The flange is the ring that bolts the toilet to the floor and connects it to the drain. When it is cracked, rusted or sits too low, the wax ring cannot seal and water leaks. Repair rings and flange kits fix many of these. A rotted floor under the flange is a larger repair, and the plumber should tell you what they find.

Tank repairs and installation

Tank parts wear out. Flappers, fill valves, flush valves and supply lines are cheap to replace and bring a running toilet back under control. If a tank cracks, the toilet needs replacement. A new tank and bowl set is also an option when the toilet is old and uses a lot of water.

Installing a new toilet

1

Remove the old toilet

The water is shut off, the tank and bowl are emptied and the toilet is lifted away.

2

Check the flange

The flange is cleaned and inspected, and repaired if needed.

3

Set the new toilet

A new wax ring or seal is placed and the toilet is bolted down level.

4

Connect and test

The supply line is connected, the tank is filled and the toilet is flushed for leaks.

When the clog is bigger than the toilet

A toilet that clogs again and again, or a bathroom where a toilet, a sink and a tub drain slowly together, has a problem further down the line. A drain cleaning visit or a look at the main sewer line finds it.

Common questions

Do you handle toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Sachse?

Yes. The 24/7 line answers at any hour. Close the shutoff valve first, then call and say the toilet is overflowing or leaking.

How do I stop a toilet from running?

Lift the tank lid and check the flapper and the chain. A worn flapper or a chain that is too short is a common cause.

Can I use a chemical cleaner in a clogged toilet?

No. Cleaners do not clear toilet clogs reliably and leave a hazard for the plumber. A plunger or an auger is safer.

Why does my toilet rock?

The floor may be uneven, the closet bolts may be loose, or the flange may be damaged. The plumber can tell which.

Should I replace an old toilet?

If it clogs, leaks or uses a lot of water, replacement often pays for itself over time.
