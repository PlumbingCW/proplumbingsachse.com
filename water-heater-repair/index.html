A failing water heater picks bad moments. It runs out of hot water on a cold morning, or it starts leaking while the family is asleep. Our 24/7 emergency line is open for both, and a licensed plumber can be on the schedule the same day when appointments allow.

Water heater repair in Sachse, TX covers gas and electric tank heaters, the thermostats and heating elements that run them, the burner assembly and igniter on gas models, and the valves and fittings that connect to your plumbing.

If the tank is leaking from the body or the base, shut off the water at the cold supply valve on top, switch off power at the breaker or turn the gas control to off, and call right away.

Leaking water heater: what to do first

Shut off the cold supply

The valve is on the pipe that enters the top of the tank. Turning it clockwise stops water from refilling a leaking tank.

Cut power or gas

Flip the breaker for an electric heater. On gas, turn the control knob to off. Never go near a gas smell, leave the home and call the gas utility.

Open a hot tap

A hot water faucet opened somewhere in the home lets pressure out and slows the leak.

Protect the floor

Move boxes and stored items away. A leak from the tank body usually means the tank has failed, not just a fitting.

No hot water: gas versus electric

Gas heater, no heat

A pilot that will not stay lit, a failing thermocouple or a dirty burner are the common causes. A cracked flue or poor combustion air is a safety issue, so the plumber checks it too.

Electric heater, no heat

A tripped high-limit switch, a burned-out upper or lower element or a failed thermostat leave the tank cold. A multimeter tests each part in minutes.

Warm but not hot

A single failed element in an electric heater, a thermostat set too low or sediment on the tank bottom shrinks the hot water supply.

Hot water runs out fast

A broken dip tube mixes cold and hot water in the tank. Sediment or a failed lower element can do the same.

Leaks: where the water comes from

A leak from a fitting or the temperature and pressure relief valve is a repair. A leak from the tank body is a failure, and no part fixes it. The plumber traces the drip to its source before recommending a repair or a replacement.

Many leak calls come from the valve at the side or the drain valve at the bottom. A new valve costs far less than a new tank, which is why a diagnosis matters before a decision.

Sounds, smells and discolored water

Popping or rumbling means sediment is trapped under the heating element or burner. Rusty or brown hot water suggests the tank lining is failing, and a rotten egg smell often points to the anode rod reacting with bacteria. Each one gets a different fix, and some are signs that the tank is near the end of its life.

Hard water and tank life in Sachse

The North Texas Municipal Water District, which supplies Sachse, describes its treated water as moderately hard. Minerals settle on the tank bottom and on heating elements, and over time they shorten element life and reduce efficiency.

Flushing a tank each year, checking the anode rod and testing the pressure relief valve are cheap ways to keep a heater going. A water softener or filter is one more option if mineral buildup keeps coming back, and a test of your tap shows how hard your water really is.

Common questions

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Sachse?

Yes. The 24/7 line answers after hours and on weekends. If the tank is leaking, shut off the cold supply and the power or gas first, then call and say it is leaking so the plumber is booked for the earliest slot.

Is it safe to relight a pilot myself?

If the pilot goes out once and relights, it may be a draft. If it keeps going out, or you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility before anything else.

Why is my water heater making popping noises?

Sediment sits under the water, and steam bubbles push through it. A flush often helps, but a heavily scaled tank may be near the end of its life.

Can a leaking tank be repaired?

A leak from a fitting or valve can be repaired. A leak from the tank body cannot, and the heater needs replacement.

How long should a water heater last?

Many last 8 to 12 years, depending on water quality and maintenance. If yours is at that age and failing, replacement is often the better value.
